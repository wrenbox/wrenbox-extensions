Why we forget most of what we read

Long read, 9 minutes

Most readers finish an article feeling they understood it. A week later, little of it remains. The problem is rarely comprehension; it is that nothing asks us to retrieve what we read. Recognition feels like knowing, but it fades quickly.

Researchers have repeated the same finding for decades. Pulling an idea back out of memory, even imperfectly, strengthens it far more than reading it again. Highlighting alone does little. What helps is returning to the highlights later and testing yourself on them.

That suggests a simple habit: mark only what you would want to explain to a friend, write one line about why it matters, and revisit those notes a day, a week and a month later. The marks become prompts rather than decoration.

None of this needs special software, but it does need your highlights to be there when you come back. A highlight that disappears is a promise broken.
