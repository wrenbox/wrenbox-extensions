Keep the lines that matter, in colour, in one place.

Bowerbirds collect colourful treasures and arrange them in their bower. Bowerline keeps the lines you collect while reading, in colour, in one place. Say it BOW-er-line: "bower" rhymes with "flower".

  • No account
  • Nothing leaves your browser
  • Free export to Obsidian, Notion and Markdown
  1. 1

    Try it right here

    Select any words below, then pick a colour. Click a highlight to change it or add a note.

    Most readers finish an article feeling they understood it. A week later, little of it remains. The problem is rarely comprehension; it is that nothing asks us to retrieve what we read. Pulling an idea back out of memory, even imperfectly, strengthens it far more than reading it again. Mark only what you would want to explain to a friend, and write one line about why it matters.

  2. 2

    Open a PDF

    Bowerline's viewer works with files on your computer and PDFs on the web. Highlights belong to the file itself, so they come back even if you move or rename it.

  3. 3

    Optional: show highlights automatically

    Out of the box, Bowerline only runs on a page when you ask: click its icon, press Alt+Shift+H, or right-click and choose "Highlight with Bowerline". Turn this on and your highlights reappear by themselves when you revisit a page.

    What will Chrome's permission prompt say?

    Chrome will ask to let Bowerline "Read and change all your data on all websites". That is Chrome's standard wording for any extension that runs on the pages you visit. Bowerline uses it for one thing: finding your saved highlights on the page and drawing them. It never reads pages for any other reason, never changes their content, and never sends anything anywhere: there is no Bowerline server.

Your highlights live only in this browser. Back them up any time from Settings.
